How a content type is built: some of the ten, plus its own

The ten shared criteria are a library, not a checklist applied to everything. Each content type takes only the ones that make sense for it, adds a few of its own, and sets all the weights so they add to 100.

So “answer up front” (U1) means the same thing everywhere — but it is worth 14 points on a blog, 10 on a solution page, and nothing at all on a news release, where a news-specific criterion does that job instead.

This is the single most misunderstood part of the standard. The shared criteria are not a universal checklist that every page must pass; they are a library that each content type draws from, and the weights are what make the score fit that type of page.
