[hidden] – Select the country where the data center will be located. Location affects capital costs due to regional differences in labor rates, equipment costs, and construction practices. All cost values are displayed in the local currency. Location does not affect cooling sizing.

[hidden] – Use the slider to adjust the average fully burdened installation and design labor rate in the selected local currency. This rate is applied to labor-related portions of the cost model and is intended to represent an overall project average. Actual labor costs vary by trade, skill level, activity. The labor rate does not affect equipment/material costs or core & shell costs.

[hidden] - Use the slider to enter the core and shell cost per square foot (or square meter), representing the cost of constructing the building structure and exterior envelope. Core and shell costs typically include items such as the building structure, foundations, exterior walls, roof, and associated building construction. Total core and shell costs are calculated based on the estimated gross facility area, including IT, electrical, and mechanical spaces. Core & shell costs do not include land acquisition costs. For additional guidance on building construction costs, users may refer to industry benchmarking resources such as RLB Construction Cost Calculator linked below. Estimates from such resources should be used only as a reference when selecting a core & shell value.

[hidden] – Indicate in kW, how much power the data center IT load is projected to consume. Type in a value between 100 kW and 100,000 kW (100 MW).

[hidden] – Select the percentage of total IT capacity that is air-cooled IT equipment. The minimum value is 10% to account for complementary air-cooled loads, such as networking racks, even when all servers are planned to be liquid cooled.

[hidden] – The percentage of total IT capacity that is liquid-cooled IT equipment is calculated using the air-cooled capacity selected above, so that the sum is 100%. Note, the calculations assume 10% of this liquid cooled IT capacity will be treated as air-cooled load, based on a heat capture ratio of 90%.

[hidden] – Type the average rack density in kW per rack for the air-cooled IT equipment. Values between 1 kW/rack and 50 kW/rack are allowed. Density impacts the cost of infrastructure subsystems, as well as the number of racks and space required. Rack counts are estimated using the specified average rack density; actual deployments may contain a mix of rack densities, but the tool assumes a uniform average density for planning purposes.

[hidden] – Type the average rack density in kW per rack for the liquid-cooled IT equipment. Values between 40 kW/rack and 200 kW/rack are allowed. Density impacts the cost of infrastructure subsystems, as well as the number of racks and space required. Rack counts are estimated using the specified average rack density; actual deployments may contain a mix of rack densities, but the tool assumes a uniform average density for planning purposes. NVIDIA Blackwell-based systems commonly range from about 100 – 150+ kW/rack depending on rack configuration. NVIDIA Vera Rubin platform variants, including Max-Q and Max-P configurations, are expected to approach or exceed 200 kW/rack. The current upper limit of 200 kW/rack was selected to encompass the majority of liquid-cooled AI data center deployments being planned today and represents a practical upper bound for comparative infrastructure planning. As AI hardware continues to evolve, rack densities are expected to increase and this limit may be revised in future releases.

[hidden] – Select the approach used for final power distribution to the IT racks: overhead busway with tap-off units to the racks, remote power panels (RPPs) with whips to the racks, or modular PDUs (power distribution units) directly feeding the racks. White paper 129 linked below provides explanations of the differences of these approaches.

[hidden] – Select between Lithium-ion and VRLA battery types. VRLA are valve-regulated lead acid batteries. Li-ion batteries for UPSs are LMO-NMC chemistry. White paper 229 linked below explains the differences between these two UPS battery technologies.

[hidden] – Select redundancy levels for each of the key power subsystems: IT distribution, UPSs, generators, MV to LV switchgear, and HV to MV switchgear. The HV to MV switchgear is only included for data centers of 5 MW or greater. UPS redundancy is defined at the UPS unit level, not the internal module level. Note that choices in power redundancy influence the design of other infrastructure systems, such as fire suppression /detection and security.

[hidden] White paper 229, Battery Technology for Data Centers: VRLA vs. Li-ion

[hidden] White paper 129, Comparing Data Center Power Distribution Architectures

[hidden] The cooling system design has a significant influence on capital costs. Complete this tab only when a portion of the IT load is specified as liquid cooled on Tab 1. The tool estimates costs at the infrastructure subsystem level and does not model detailed component-level design choices, such as specific valve types, manifold designs, or other equipment selections.

[hidden] – Select from the available liquid-cooling architectures. Architectures may either share cooling infrastructure with the air-cooled loads or use a dedicated system for only the liquid-cooled loads. Dedicated systems include facility water systems (FWS) with air-cooled chillers, water-cooled chillers, or without chillers. Direct expansion (DX) is not a selectable option for dedicated liquid-cooling systems because it is not an optimized approach for liquid-cooled loads.

[hidden] – Select the outdoor heat rejection system. Available options vary based on the selected architecture. If a shared architecture was selected, this field is auto-populated with the air-cooled heat rejection selection on the prior tab.

[hidden] – Select the coolant distribution unit (CDU) configuration of direct-to-chip with liquid-to-liquid or liquid-to-air. With liquid-to-liquid CDUs, heat is transferred from the technology cooling system (TCS) loop serving the IT equipment to the facility water system (FWS) loop through the CDU's heat exchanger. With liquid-to-air CDUs, heat is transferred from the TCS loop to the surrounding air within the CDU and is then removed by the CRACs or CRAHs serving the space. Liquid-to-air CDUs are only available with DX architectures. Note, Liquid-to-air CDUs are also referred to as Heat Dissipation Unit (HDU).

[hidden] – Select either floor-mounted or rack-mounted CDUs. Note, only the floor-mounted format is available when liquid-to-air CDUs are used, or when rack densities are above 100 kW/rack with liquid-to-liquid CDUs.

[hidden] – Select redundancy levels for each of the key cooling subsystems: TCS piping, CDUs, facility piping, and heat rejection. Manifolds are always 1N. Redundancy selections also influence the supporting infrastructure, such as fire detection and suppression systems and security systems. Note, if a shared heat rejection system is selected, the redundancy options for heat rejection will be greyed out, as they are chosen on the Air cooling tab.

[hidden] The cooling system design has a significant influence on capital costs. If 100% of the IT capacity is air cooled, only this tab applies. If a mix of air- and liquid-cooled IT is specified on Tab 1, complete both the Air Cooling and Liquid Cooling tabs.

[hidden] – Select between air-cooled chiller, water-cooled chiller, chilled water without a chiller, or direct expansion (DX). Air-cooled chillers reject heat directly to outdoor air. Water-cooled chillers reject heat through cooling towers or dry coolers. Chilled water without a chiller assumes chilled water is supplied without an on-site chiller plant, with heat rejected through cooling towers or dry coolers. Direct expansion (DX) rejects heat directly to outdoor air through refrigerant-based systems without a central chilled water plant.

[hidden] – Select the outdoor heat rejection system. Available options vary based on the selected architecture.

[hidden] – When direct expansion is selected, this field is set to CRACs (computer room air conditioners). For all other architectures, it is set to CRAHs (computer room air handlers).

[hidden] – Select the deployment format of the coolers. Options include room-level, row-level, and rack-level configurations. Note, the rack-level option is only available for CRAHs which take the form of rear-door heat exchangers (RDHXs). Room-level systems typically use perimeter units or, in larger deployments, fanwalls. The tool assumes a uniform air cooling deployment strategy across the data hall and does not model a mix of cooling formats (for example, some racks with RDHXs and others without).

[hidden] – Select between hot-aisle contained, cold-aisle contained, or no containment. Note, above rack densities of 8 kW/rack with room-level, or above 20 kW/rack with row-level, the “no containment” option is not available. Rack-level coolers (RDHX) do not use aisle containment. Hot-aisle containment includes dedicated hot-air return infrastructure (e.g., ductwork or return plenums), resulting in higher capital costs than cold-aisle containment.

[hidden] – Select redundancy levels for each of the key air cooling subsystems: Coolers, facility piping, and heat rejection. Redundancy selections also influence the supporting infrastructure, such as fire detection and suppression systems and security systems.

[hidden] Check or uncheck each box corresponding to the subsystems you want included in the capital cost estimate. All are checked by default except raised floors, as they are becoming less common in data center designs.

[hidden] HV-to-MV distribution is only available for data centers with an IT capacity of 5 MW or greater.

[hidden] TCS equipment (CDUs, piping, and manifolds) can only be selected when a portion of the IT load is specified as liquid cooled.

[hidden] Detailed breakdown

[hidden] IT:

[hidden] – Servers are traditional designs with internal power supplies (not OCP-style).

[hidden] – For liquid-cooled IT, 10% of the heat is removed with air, based on the assumed heat capture ratio of 90% (simplifying assumption of the cost model). Actual heat capture ratios vary by server & cooling technology, and may be higher than 90% for some deployments.

[hidden] Power:

[hidden] – The electrical room size starts with a baseline of 2.57 sq ft/kW and then scales based on UPS redundancy.

[hidden] – The HV to MV switchgear is included for data centers of 5 MW or greater.

[hidden] – The HV to MV switchgear, when present, is assumed to be located outdoors, and doesn’t impact grey space footprint.

[hidden] – Separate upstream IT and mechanical power trains are not modeled.

[hidden] – Li-ion batteries for UPSs are LMO-NMC chemistry.

[hidden] Cooling:

[hidden] – The climate used to estimate the cooling requirements is St Louis, MO, USA.

[hidden] – Liquid cooling cannot be 100% of data center racks; A minimum of 10% of the total IT capacity is from air cooled IT racks (e.g., networking and storage racks).

[hidden] – When rear-door heat exchangers (RDHX) are used for air cooled racks, RDHXs are also included on liquid cooled racks to handle the 10% of heat rejected to air.

[hidden] – All chillers have variable frequency drives (VFD).

[hidden] – Containment is required above rack densities of 8 kW/rack with room-based coolers, and above 20 kW/rack with row-based coolers.

[hidden] – Liquid-to-air CDUs are only in floor-mounted form factor.

[hidden] – Coolers to support the air portion of liquid cooled racks adopt the same redundancy as what is defined by the user for air-cooled cooler redundancy.

[hidden] Other:

[hidden] – Capacity is fully built on Day 1 and does not model phased growth or modular/pod-based deployment strategies.

[hidden] – When selected, raised floor is added only for the IT space with air-cooled racks.

[hidden] – Air-cooled and liquid-cooled IT spaces are calculated independently. Actual deployments may share aisles and infrastructure, resulting in a smaller total IT footprint.

[hidden] – When dedicated chillers are selected for liquid-cooled loads, chiller capacities are higher due to higher usable chilled water temperatures.

[hidden] – Fire, security, and management system redundancy is 1N if the average redundancy of power and cooling systems is N or N+1; and 2N if the average redundancy of power and cooling systems is 2N or 2(N+1).

[hidden] This tool estimates the capital cost implications of key physical infrastructure design decisions for both traditional and AI data center deployments. By defining parameters such as IT load capacity, IT cooling approach (air-cooled or liquid-cooled), redundancy levels, rack densities, and major power and cooling characteristics, the tool projects the number of racks required, estimated floor space, and the associated capital costs.

[hidden] Capital cost estimates include rough-order-of-magnitude values for the materials, installation labor, and engineering and design activities associated with each aspect of the physical infrastructure. The results are intended to illustrate the impact of different design choices and support early planning activities. They should not be used as formal quotations, budget approvals, or procurement estimates.
