[hidden] An edge UPS fleet is a group of UPSs that provide power & availability of IT solutions in locations that are not located in a centralized or regional data center. An example would be a network rack in the back-office of a retail store chain, healthcare office, or even UPS & related accessories supporting educational applications. In assessing the costs of managing a fleet, it is first essential to define the attributes of it:

[hidden] Site quantity – Enter the number of distributed sites where the UPSs reside.

[hidden] UPS rating (VA) – You can select up to three different UPS sizes that exist in your edge sites.

[hidden] UPSs per site – For each UPS size you defined, enter how many you have per site.

[hidden] UPS age distribution – For each UPS size you defined, provide the mix of ages of your assets. Each row will total 100%. This distribution is important because the cost of Vendor / Partner service contracts vary based on the warranty status of your UPSs. In-warranty contracts often already include parts coverage due to factory warranty policy. Older, out-of-warranty UPS systems no longer carry parts coverage, and that cost will be represented in out-of-warranty pricing. For UPSs containing VRLA batteries, typically the factory warranty is for the first the three years of installation. This age distribution also impacts the failure rate of UPSs in your fleet.

[hidden] Truck roll cost - Reference article "Reduce your Truck Rolls Costs: The Surprising Solution"

[hidden] When managing a UPS fleet yourself, there are costs associated with allocating staff as well as technician / travel costs for repairs or parts replacement (i.e. batteries). In this section, the details about your expected operational costs are used to calculate the total costs when managing the fleet yourself.

[hidden] Location – Select a country so that the analysis can be localized based on costs and currency.

[hidden] Number of staff managing fleet – Enter the number of personnel in your organization that have responsibility for managing and/or remediating the UPS fleet. With a higher volume of distributed infrastructure, it is possible that more than one person is responsible for managing & maintaining the fleet. A staff member that spends a fraction of their time on these tasks should still be counted.

[hidden] Percentage of each staff time allocated – Enter the percentage of time each staff member spends on these tasks. Depending on the size of the UPSs, number of UPSs, and size & structure of the organization; the percentage of time your personnel spend on managing the fleet is likely to vary.

[hidden] Average annual staff member cost – Enter the fully loaded cost (salary + benefits) for each staff member, on average. This value, along with the prior values for number of staff and % of time allocated are used to calculate the staff expense. In the United States, IT operations technicians have a typical base salary raning from $43k-$70k, according to Salary.com. Benefits are valued at approximately 30% of total compensation, so this puts a typical total compensation for IT operations staff at $100k per full time person.

[hidden] Technician & travel cost – Enter the average cost incurred when you dispatch a service technician for a repair. This is often referred to as a “truck roll”. A typical cost for this in the United States is $1000 but can vary based on size of UPS and distance to travel for technician(s). The link below provides a source for the default value.

[hidden] Percentage of failures resulting in downtime – Some failure events result in downtime, while others do not. For instance, an alert that your UPS battery is approaching it’s end of life is a pro-active alarm that, if acted upon in a timely fashion, can be resolved with no downtime. Some events are unexpected and lead to downtime. When managing a large fleet of UPSs yourself, the remote nature of a distributed fleet can lead to missed alerts and/or longer time frames to resolve issues. This can increase the percentage of failures that lead to downtime. The analysis defaults to 50% of failures for a self-managed fleet, but can be adjusted.

[hidden] Average duration per downtime event (MTTR) – When a downtime event occurs, the Mean Time to Recover or MTTR is the average length of time your critical loads are unavailable, based on the time it takes to repair the system. It often includes time to diagnose, time to repair and time to test. Generally, the larger the UPS, the longer it takes to recover from a failure because of the complexity involved with the system. You can calculate your MTTR by adding up the total time spent on past repairs during any given period and then dividing that time by the number of repairs.

[hidden] Cost of downtime per minute – When your UPS loads are down, what does it cost your business? According to independent data protection and security research firm, Ponemon, the largest share of downtime cost is business disruption—a category that includes reputational damage and customer churn. Revenue loss took second place in the firm’s research. And the third largest financial pain associated with incidents was end-user productivity.

[hidden] Downtime avoided with Vendor / Partner-Managed – Having a vendor or partner remotely monitor and manage your fleet 24x7 helps to reduce downtime events because subject matter experts are attending to your fleet around the clock and predictive alarms are addressed before downtime problems occur. Some downtime events, however, cannot be avoided. For example, a UPS with a fresh battery and 30 minutes of runtime capability will still eventually lose power during an extended power event such as a local power outage, or natural disaster. This percentage of unavoidable downtime should be considered, as setting it to ‘zero’ may result in missed expectations of service delivery.

[hidden] For purposes of this calculator, the term “failure” is defined as an event requiring physical human intervention to resolve the issue. These failure events, although inclusive of critical component failures, are driven by the battery service life, since VRLA batteries have a typical life expectancy of

[hidden] 3-6 years. This is why the default values increase so drastically above 3 years.

[hidden] A summary of the total cost for one year (based on current age distribution of assets) is provided for both a self managed fleet and a vendor / partner-managed fleet. Downtime (hours) are also provided for each fleet management approach. A final summary of savings or premium is presented for both costs and downtime reduction.

[hidden] “Vendor / partner-managed” – With a vendor / partner-managed UPS fleet, annual costs include the predictable cost for the service contract by the 3rd party provider (such as Schneider Electric Field Services) to monitor & maintain your distributed IT infrastructure fleet on behalf of your organization, plus the cost of unavoidable downtime. Choosing this approach minimizes the amount of time spent on administrative & remediation activities associated with distributed IT infrastructure by your own staff; and downtime is significantly reduced due to visibility through monitoring & reducing failure points before events occur.

[hidden] “Self-managed” – This is the variable annual cost associated with administrative and remediation responsibilities by the organization. Depending on the level of visibility via monitoring, the organization is more proactive or reactive in maintaining their fleet. For organizations that more reactive, downtime events result in longer times to resolution and therefore greater costs. All associated costs related to deploying resources to sites and taking away from productive work will contribute to these costs and may be highly variable from year-to-year.

[hidden] The purpose of this tool is to show how much managing a distributed fleet of IT infrastructure (single phase UPSs) can cost an organization compared to paying someone else to do it for you. As the user inputs details regarding the ages of the UPSs in the fleet, results are calculated based upon a dynamic model that considers device count, rating (VA), downtime related costs, and the time spent by resources managing & maintaining the fleet.

[hidden] This is a model for typical distributed UPSs found in different environments such as retails stores, healthcare facilities, school campuses, and more. The primary value of this tool is to evaluate the comparative costs of monitoring & maintaining approaches; specifically, manage the fleet yourself or have someone (a vendor or partner) do it for you.

[hidden] The amount of time that employees spend managing & responding to UPS fleet alarms and repair requirements, as well as costs associated with potential downtime are important quantifiable variables that influence the decision. This tool help provide visibility into these real costs associated with managing a fleet yourself. Over time these costs typically increase as UPSs begin to age and batteries require replacements. As the typical fleet matures, and/ or becomes more mixed in terms of age, and UPS rating; the burden on in-house staff tends to increase.

[hidden] TradeOff Tools™ are based upon a series of mathematical models developed by the Schneider Electric Science Center. Fundamental research principles for development of these tools can be accessed via the Schneider Electric white paper library.

[hidden] The assumptions made in this tool are based upon findings from discussions with our customers about their distributed IT / Edge computing environments. Some of the assumptions are based on the VA capacity of the UPS systems installed For example a 750VA UPS supporting a single cash register will have less associated costs of downtime compared to a 5000VA UPS that is centrally supporting store operations in a back office. These assumptions can be changed by the user based upon individual circumstances. Key assumptions are as follows:

[hidden] • UPSs have internal VRLA batteries (no external batteries or Li-ion batteries were considered)

[hidden] • UPSs are remotely monitorable

[hidden] • UPSs are covered under warranty for 3 years (vendor / partner services are less expensive in those years because parts are covered in the warranty)

[hidden] • All out-of-warranty repairs assume a parts cost equivalent to a replacement battery

[hidden] • UPSs across a fleet are located within the same country
