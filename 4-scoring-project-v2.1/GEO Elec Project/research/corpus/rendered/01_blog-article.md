# Modular, prefabricated data centers enter the AI era and boldly go where no data centers have gone before

- October 13, 2026

- 96 views

- 5 min read

- Steven Carlini

I have been strategically involved in the modular data center industry for over 30 years – yes, the industry has been doing this for a long time. However, the exponential demand for prefabrication is gaining significant momentum now. Spoiler alert: the race to build AI factory data centers is driving this momentum. But why the need for prefabrication in AI data centers? Let’s take a look.

## A slow start

AI is the latest technology wave hitting data centers. A major data center buildout took place during the dot-com boom (1997–2000), followed by a wave for cloud computing in the mid-2000s to 2010s. While there were some “all-in-ones,” packaged chillers and power skids, these data centers were primarily “stick built” or assembled from pieces procured through a bid-spec process. The data center plan, design, bid, build, and commission cycle involved architects and consulting engineers to create custom designs. Then they had to send out specifications and at least three qualified vendors had to bid on the pieces. It’s not a surprise that this process struggled significantly with “assemble-to-order” data centers and prefabricated data centers. Looking back, it’s a wonder anything got built.

That’s not the only reason modular data centers were unpopular initially. The stigmatism of the initial designs being housed in rusty, old, repurposed shipping containers with high costs and long lead times was problematic. Additionally, initial deployments triggered door, stairs, and ramps for unforeseen access requirements. Trouble driving the large modules down some streets, especially in cities, and a lack of cranes on-site were part of the learning curve.

## Challenges of the AI era

Today’s data center AI factories are significantly different and even more challenging than cloud versions of data centers. They require a new planning through commissioning process. One difference is the size and scale because AI factory campuses can be up to 10 gigawatts. Therefore, high power equipment scheduling and primary power access or construction are usually on the critical path. The power densities per rack are close to 250 kW vs 10-20 of cloud data centers. Cooling is now hybrid (air and liquid) but primarily liquid cooled where cloud data centers didn’t usually have any liquid cooling.

These criteria – power capacity and scale and high density liquid cooling – are causing data center operators considerable planning, design, and construction challenges. But prefabrication will significantly improve the probability of the AI factory data centers being deployed on-schedule and operating at the desired performance.

## Power Capacity and Scale

Prefabricating Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS) and switchgear systems onto skids or enclosures delivers numerous advantages including:

- Faster delivery times and project deployment due to site prep being done in advance

- The ability to roll solutions into place

- The reassurance of factory-tested quality

- Lower labor costs vs doing in the field

- Fewer risks

Building these power units off-site cuts on-site labor and avoids weather delays. Skilled electrician labor at the site is becoming a significant concern for deploying these power systems from a time perspective.

## High Density Liquid Cooling

Densities are getting seriously high with the new AMD Helios 445 racks running at 246 kW per rack and the NVIDIA Vera Rubin running at 227 kW per rack. Liquid cooling at scale came on the scene three years ago starting at around 70 kW per rack and it’s growing exceedingly fast. The expertise needed at the rack and the AI cluster in an IT Pod includes manifolds, hot and cold liquid plumbing to the servers, and the CDUs. CDU programming and management is considerable.

All this is happening while significantly more power connections are coming onto the busway and into the rack. The new innovation is the AI factory modular IT Pod, which is designed for specific high density AI clusters. In an AI cluster, the power and cooling components sit above the AI racks in an overhead metal superstructure. The AI Pod is innovative because it not only houses the AI servers, it includes all of the pieces for the power and cooling – busway, power distribution and connectors, main hot and cold piping, and hot and cold piping to the servers. The major pieces are assembled in a factory, packed flat, then they are assembled in the IT room into the Pod. This approach is quite different than a skid or enclosure. It reduces design and deployment time and brings prebuilt modules into a space they’ve never been before – the AI factory IT room.

## Modular, prefabrication is a necessity today and will be in the future

So, there you have it: site preparation can be done in parallel of power module and IT Pod production. The power modules can be shipped to the site, connected, and start delivering power to the IT room. The CDU can be connected to the facility water loop. The IT Pod is put together in place, then connect the IT Pod liquid loop to the CDU and power from the power module, roll the accelerated IT compute into place and connect the power plugs and piping connectors, and start manufacturing intelligence!

Modular data centers may not be new, but challenges driven by the latest accelerated compute AI clusters are forcing accelerated change in the way data centers are designed and deployed. While prefabrication could have been seen as a luxury a couple of decades ago, at this scale of deployment and with these challenging densities coupled with a major shortage of tradespeople, prefabrication is a necessity today. And it will be even more of a necessity in the future.
