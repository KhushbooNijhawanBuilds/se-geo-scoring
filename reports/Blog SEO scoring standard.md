# Score blog SEO on evidence, not folklore

Schneider Electric can score blog SEO as rigorously as its GEO score by keeping the page score to what page text and HTML can show. That means nine criteria worth 100 points, binary checks quoted from the page, gates for spam and broken promises, and capped penalties. Technical items (indexability, canonical, hreflang, HTTPS, Core Web Vitals) and off-page items (backlinks, click behaviour, site authority) stay out of the points. They become a pass/fail eligibility check, or site-level context labelled "needs data". This split follows the evidence. The strongest measured correlate of ranking is link authority, and it is off-page **([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/search-engine-ranking))**. Among content factors, text relevance comes first **([Semrush](https://semrush.com/blog/ranking-factors-semrush-study))**. Google's own self-assessment questions are the best official basis for judging content **([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content))**. Popular plugin checks such as word-count ladders, keyword density and "power words" have no support in the notes, and Google rejects several of them outright **([Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide))**. The proposed SEO score overlaps heavily with the existing GEO blog rubric. GEO already covers intent, structure, expertise, freshness and entity consistency. It misses keyword-in-title hygiene, title length, meta description, slug, alt text, internal-link architecture and structured data. The two pull in different directions in only a few places: keyword placement, length, and how much CTR-oriented titles are still worth now that AI Overviews cut clicks by roughly a third to three fifths **([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/))**, **([Seer](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update))**. One caution applies throughout. Much of the empirical base is vendor correlation work, and several official pages were cited from memory rather than re-read, so the score should claim to measure quality and hygiene, not to predict rankings.

## Most SEO factors can be judged from the page itself

The factors split cleanly once you ask what evidence each one needs. **On-page/content factors** can be judged from the rendered text and HTML. They include the title tag, H1, meta description, heading structure, intent match, topical coverage, sourcing, byline and author bio, visible dates, anchor text, alt text, the URL slug and Article structured data. They also include the spam patterns Google names as content problems: keyword stuffing, hidden text, scraped or scaled low-value text, thin affiliation and misleading functionality **([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies))**. **Technical factors** need a fetch or crawl. They include HTTP status, robots and noindex, canonical resolution, hreflang reciprocity, HTTPS, mobile parity, intrusive interstitials, Core Web Vitals and cloaking. Lighthouse checks these, and its HTTP-status and robots audits need a live request **([Lighthouse](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/seo/meta-description))**. **Off-page factors** cannot be seen on the page at all. They include backlinks and referring domains, NavBoost-style click data, Chrome signals and site authority. Sworn trial testimony confirmed NavBoost exists, and the 2024 documentation leak confirmed some of these attributes exist, but neither gives weights **([Hobo Web](https://www.hobo-web.co.uk/navboost-how-google-uses-large-scale-user-interaction-data-to-rank-websites/))**, **([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Google_Search_documentation_leak))**.

A few factors sit on the boundary. Canonical and hreflang tags are written in the HTML but only mean something across the site. Duplicate keyphrase targeting and cannibalisation need a cross-page view. Competitor-relative depth needs SERP data, which is how Surfer works: it compares a draft against the top 20 results **([Surfer](https://docs.surferseo.com/en/articles/5700317-what-is-content-score))**. The score below treats tag presence as on-page and tag correctness as technical eligibility.

## Strong, weak and mythical: what the evidence supports

| Factor | Class | Evidence strength | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Topical relevance and coverage of sub-questions | C | Moderate–strong (correlation; "text relevance" #1) ([Semrush](https://semrush.com/blog/ranking-factors-semrush-study)) | Heavy weight |
| Intent satisfaction / query answered | C | Strong as a mechanism (NavBoost rewards satisfying clicks; weights unknown) ([AIOSEO](https://aioseo.com/google-search-algorithm-leak/)) | Heavy weight + gate |
| Accurate, non-exaggerated title/H1 | C | Official ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)) | Weighted + gate |
| Title length 50–60 chars, no repetition | C | Observed behaviour: 61.6% of titles rewritten; 99.9% over 70 chars ([Zyppy](https://zyppy.com/seo/google-title-rewrites/)) | Binary check |
| Keyword in title | C | Weak within top 10, high prevalence ([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/search-engine-ranking)) | Hygiene check, not a gradient |
| Byline, author bio, sourcing, method | C | Official, as trust evidence; "E-E-A-T is not a ranking factor" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)) | Weighted |
| Original information and analysis | C | Official, plus leak attributes (`contentEffort`, read by practitioners) ([Hobo Web](https://www.hobo-web.co.uk/the-contenteffort-attribute-the-helpful-content-system-and-e-e-a-t-is-gemini-behind-the-hcu/)) | Weighted |
| Descriptive anchors, alt text, readable slug | C | Official guidance; no ranking study found ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)) | Light weight |
| Article JSON-LD (author.url, dates) | C | Official recommendation; "no required properties" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article)) | Light weight |
| Spam-policy violations | C/T | Official, a hard fail ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)) | Gates/penalties |
| Core Web Vitals, page speed | T | Weak tiebreaker ([Perficient](https://www.perficient.com/insights/research-hub/impact-of-core-web-vitals-on-ranking)), ([AWR](https://advancedwebranking.com/blog/core-web-vitals-study)) | Site context |
| Indexability, HTTPS, canonical, hreflang | T | Prerequisite ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)), ([Google hreflang](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions)) | Eligibility check |
| Backlinks / referring domains | O | Strongest correlate ([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/search-engine-ranking)) | Site context, "needs data" |

The myths to exclude are just as clear. Google says the keywords meta tag is not used, that content length "alone doesn't matter", that keywords in the domain or URL have "hardly any effect", and that heading order and count do not matter for Search **([Google SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide))**. It also says it has "no preferred word count" **([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content))**. Word count is spread evenly across the top 10 **([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/search-engine-ranking))**. That makes Rank Math's ladder, which gives full marks only above 2,500 words, a padding incentive **([Rank Math](https://rankmath.com/kb/score-100-in-tests/))**. The same goes for its power-word and sentiment title tests. Keyword density bands (Yoast's 0.5–3%, Rank Math's 1–1.5%) have no supporting study in the notes. Their status as a myth rests on consensus, not on a test. Content-optimisation scores correlate only weakly with rank, at about 0.17–0.30 **([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/seo-content-score-study/))**. llms.txt and special AI markup are unnecessary **([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features))**. FAQ rich results are now restricted to well-known government and health sites **([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes))**. And Google says outright that it does not use third-party tool scores **([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-we-dont-use-third-party-seo-tool-scores-for-rankings/493956))**. That is why the score below publishes every check and does not claim to predict rankings.

The notes also flag real gaps. No sourced evidence was found on keyword-in-first-paragraph, keyword-in-URL, Flesch readability, alt text, outbound links, internal anchor text or meta-description CTR. Readability's link to rank is mixed **([NTU repository](https://nanyangtechnologicaluniv.demo.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/variable-quality-and-readability-of-patient-oriented-websites-on-))**. Several Google pages (snippets, link best practices, publication dates, the Quality Rater Guidelines) and the Bing guidelines were cited from memory, not re-read. The specific targets of the 2025–2026 core updates are undocumented. These items get light weights or binary hygiene treatment so that the score's result does not hinge on them.

## A 100-point blog SEO score built like the GEO score

Each criterion has five to six binary checks, and each check must be justified by quoting the page element (the title string, the H1, a sentence, an anchor). The level runs from 0 to 5 and is set by the share of checks passed: 0% = 0, up to 20% = 1, up to 40% = 2, up to 60% = 3, up to 80% = 4, above 80% = 5. Criterion points = weight × level ÷ 5. Next, apply gates (the score cannot exceed the cap), then subtract penalties (total capped at −10). Bands: **90+ Excellent, 70–89 Good, 40–69 Average, <40 Poor.**

| ID | Criterion | Weight | Binary checks (quote the evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Search intent answered | 15 | Primary query identifiable from title/H1; direct answer within first 150 words; answer uses the searcher's terms; content type fits intent (how-to, explainer, comparison); main question fully resolved without leaving the page; no bait-and-switch to product pitch |
| S2 | Title, H1 and meta | 15 | `<title>` present and unique; 30–60 chars and under 70; primary topic term appears naturally; no repeated keyword or stale year; one H1 matching title intent; meta description present, specific, 70–160 chars, consistent with H1 |
| S3 | Topic coverage and focus | 15 | Covers the main sub-questions a searcher would ask next; defines key entities/terms; includes specifics (values, standards, conditions); one topic per URL; no sections off the topic; depth matched to the question, not to a word target |
| S4 | Structure and readability | 10 | Descriptive H2/H3s every major section; question-style subheads where natural; lists/tables for steps or comparisons; paragraphs mostly ≤ ~100 words; no walls of text; key facts in HTML text, not only images |
| S5 | Expertise and sourcing | 15 | Named byline; author bio/credential linked; factual claims cite sources; outbound links to authoritative primary sources; method or basis stated where relevant; no easily checked factual errors |
| S6 | Original value | 10 | First-hand data, example, case or field experience; Schneider-specific insight not available on competing pages; analysis beyond paraphrase; original visual or table; no unedited mass-produced text |
| S7 | Freshness and dates | 5 | Visible published date; visible "updated" date when revised; dates match `datePublished`/`dateModified`; no stale years/standards presented as current; update reflects substantive change |
| S8 | Links | 10 | ≥2 contextual internal links to related cluster/pillar pages; descriptive anchor text (no "click here"); links use crawlable `<a href>`; sponsored/UGC links tagged; no broken-looking or irrelevant links; a relevant next step linked |
| S9 | Media, slug and markup | 5 | Content images have descriptive alt text; images near relevant text; readable descriptive slug; Article JSON-LD present with author name + url; headline/image present in markup; markup matches visible text |

Weights total 100. They put about 70% on S1, S2, S3, S5 and S6, the criteria where official guidance and correlation evidence agree. They keep S7 and S9 light because the evidence there is thin.

**Gates (score capped):** spam-policy violation (hidden text/links, cloaking signs, scraped or scaled unedited text, doorway funnel) → 39. Query never answered → 39. Unsafe or false claim on a safety-relevant topic → 39. Title promise not delivered or exaggerated → 49. No `<title>` or no H1 → 59. Internal contradiction → 59. Key facts only in images or PDFs → 59. No visible date → 59. Thin page (no substantive answer beyond boilerplate) → 49.

**Penalties (total capped at −10):** hidden instructions or prompt injection −10; keyword stuffing in body, title or alt −3; self-ranking "best X" lists that place Schneider first without criteria −3; superseded content without notice −3; title over 70 chars or duplicate site name −2; fluff and filler intros −2; boilerplate over 40% of the text −2; placeholder or lorem text −2; vague CTAs or generic anchors −1.

**Separate eligibility check (pass/fail, not points):** returns HTTP 200; indexable (no noindex, not blocked by robots); self-referencing or correct canonical; reciprocal hreflang with x-default across se.com country paths **([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions))**; HTTPS; mobile version carries the same content; no NOCACHE or NOARCHIVE if Copilot citation is wanted **([SEJ on Bing](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/bing-adds-geo-to-official-guidelines-expands-ai-abuse-definitions/568442))**. If any of these fails, report "Not eligible" next to the content score; do not blend the two. **Site-level context (reported, "needs data"):** Core Web Vitals at LCP ≤2.5s, INP <200ms, CLS <0.1 **([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals))**, referring domains, Search Console impressions and indexing status, and keyphrase cannibalisation across posts.

## GEO already covers about two-thirds of on-page SEO

| SEO criterion | GEO coverage | Status | Relation |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 intent answered | U1 answer up front, U10 stage fit, gate "question never answered" | Covered | Agree |
| S2 title/H1/meta | ART-2 (searcher-language H1, H1/SEO title/meta consistent, promise delivered; gate 49) | Partly: no length, uniqueness, stale-year or keyword-presence check | Agree; mild differ on keyword placement |
| S3 coverage and focus | U6 fan-out coverage and focus, thin-page gate | Covered | Differ: SEO tools reward length; GEO rewards focus |
| S4 structure | U3 extractable structure, ART-1 key takeaways, ART-2 question H2s, image/PDF gate | Covered | Agree |
| S5 expertise/sourcing | U5 evidence/expertise/named author, U4 factual density, U7 neutral tone | Covered (GEO stronger) | Agree; citing sources gave +28% in GEO tests ([GEO paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3)) |
| S6 original value | ART-3 original insight | Covered | Agree |
| S7 freshness | U9 freshness and visible date, superseded penalty, no-date gate | Partly: no markup-date match | Agree |
| S8 links | U8 descriptive link text, U10 next step, vague-CTA penalty | Partly: no internal-link/cluster check | Agree |
| S9 media/slug/markup | Image/PDF gate only | Missing (alt, slug, Article JSON-LD) | Neutral for GEO |
| Spam gates | Hidden-instruction and keyword-stuffing penalties | Partly: no scraped/scaled/doorway gate | Agree |
| Eligibility (index, canonical, hreflang) | None | Missing | Agree: a prerequisite for AI features ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)) |
| CWV, backlinks | None | Missing (rightly) | Neutral; authority shapes retrieval rank |

GEO items with no SEO equivalent are U4 factual density, U7 neutral tone, ART-1 key takeaways, and the penalties for hidden instructions and self-ranking. All are harmless to SEO. Factual density and neutrality also line up with Google's trust questions. The real tensions are narrow. **Keyword placement:** SEO hygiene wants the topic term in the title, H1 and intro, while GEO penalises stuffing, which cut visibility by 9% in the GEO benchmark **([GEO paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.09735v3))**. One natural use in the title and in the first answer sentence satisfies both. **Length:** plugin culture rewards long posts, while GEO's fluff and boilerplate penalties reward concision. The evidence sides with GEO, since Google says length alone does not matter. **CTR-oriented titles:** curiosity titles can lift clicks but risk the GEO "title promise" gate. Clicks are also worth less now: Pew found people clicked a traditional result on only 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, against 15% without one **([Infodocket/Pew](https://www.infodocket.com/2025/07/22/pew-research-google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results))**. **Ranking versus citation:** only about 38% of AI Overview citations came from top-10 pages in one Ahrefs snapshot, and about 12% for ChatGPT, Gemini and Copilot **([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/search-rankings-ai-citations))**, **([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-overlap/))**. But C-SEO Bench found that a page's position in the retrieved context still matters more than most GEO tactics **([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11097))**. The two scores measure related but distinct outcomes, and neither replaces the other.

## Conclusion

Reusing GEO checks is the practical win: roughly two-thirds of the SEO content score can reuse GEO evidence, so the new SEO-only work is a small hygiene layer (title length and uniqueness, meta, slug, alt text, internal cluster links, Article markup) plus a technical eligibility check that GEO also needs and currently lacks. When the two are combined later, keep them as **two published scores on one shared check library**. That means one page parse, with each check tagged SEO, GEO or both. Run one eligibility gate for both, so a non-indexable page cannot score well on either. Use the stricter cap whenever both scores have a gate for the same issue, and keep site-level authority and CWV as context beside both scores, never inside either. Blending them into one number would hide the fact that ranking and citation now diverge. The useful management signal is a page scoring Good on one and Average on the other.

## Technical appendix

**Scoring formula.** For criterion *i* with *n* checks and *p* passes, `level = 0 if p=0 else ceil(5·p/n)`, which matches the 0/20/40/60/80% boundaries. Then `raw = Σ wᵢ·levelᵢ/5`, `capped = min(raw, min(applicable gate caps))`, and `final = max(0, capped − min(10, Σ penalties))`. Report the eligibility result and site context next to `final`.

**Evidence conventions.** Each check outputs pass/fail with a quoted span (an HTML element or a sentence) and a source tag: C (content), T (technical) or O (off-page). Checks that need a fetch, crawl, SERP data or Search Console data are emitted as "needs_data" rather than guessed, following the severity-gating and "needs_api" pattern in community audit skills **([skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh/hainrixz/claude-seo-ai/score))**. An LLM judge is acceptable for semantic checks (intent answered, coverage, originality) if it is calibrated against human labels, as Bing's labelling work showed **([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.10621v3))**.

**Thresholds and their basis.** The 30–60 and 70-character title limits come from rewrite behaviour **([Zyppy](https://zyppy.com/seo/google-title-rewrites/))**. The meta description range of 70–160 characters is a softened version of Yoast's 120–156 **([Yoast](https://yoast.com/help/yoast-seo-for-shopify-editor/))** and has no ranking evidence behind it. The ~100-word paragraph limit is adapted from the Yoast and Rank Math readability checks **([Rank Math](https://rankmath.com/kb/score-100-in-tests/))** and justified on usability and extractability grounds. The Article markup fields follow Google's recommended properties.

**Validation before rollout.** Score a sample of se.com blog posts with both rubrics and have editors check inter-rater agreement. Then compare the results with Search Console clicks and impressions and with Bing AI Performance citations as outcome checks, not training targets. Re-check the cited-from-memory Google pages, the Bing guidelines and the QRG PDF before freezing the weights. Unverified points include Schneider's actual hreflang and canonical setup and how peer blogs (Siemens, ABB, Eaton) are structured.
